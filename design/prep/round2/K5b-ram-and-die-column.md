### 0.11 The pick

Kept as K5's identity: **the tube with a loose piston over a die, pushed by a press, product falling straight
into the vessel below** — now used for every force operation of the kitchen: dice, slice, rice, press-peel,
core, split heads, form, round, dose pastes, flatten meat, strike eggs, and as the plating depositor.

Built around it, all plain and mostly bought: one heated **turning position under the column** (K2 #44, K6
#66), one 5-axis handler with a tang grip (K5 shuttle + K6 #65), a bought 4-zone induction field, a bought
countertop combi-steam oven, a bought cordless stick blender, a bought dishwasher outside the cell. Removed:
turret, carousel, face rotor, diverter, mixer crank, dock, egg module, frying book, two ring drives, wash kit,
plunger kneading, dashers, iris, duckbill, rasp can position.

## 2. The improved concept

### 2.1 Definition (one page)

K5b is **a press column standing over the one turning, heated cooking position**, with a plain 5-axis
handler, two plain induction zones, a bought countertop combi-steam oven and a port. Everything that
touches food is loose ware that goes to the household dishwasher outside the cell.

* **The column (bay A).** A square tube (100 × 100 mm, 260 mm long) stands in a die cassette that sits in
  a Ø 260 aperture of the die shelf. A loose piston lies on the food. A crosshead above, pulled down by two
  rods from twin screws under the deck (4 kN, 600 mm stroke), pushes the piston. What leaves the die falls
  straight into the pot below. Dicing uses bought push-dicer blade sets with their own pusher heads (no
  cut-off needed); slicing, portioning and grating use a **knife cage** that stands on the turning ring
  around the pot, so one revolution of P0 is one cut. The same press splits whole heads, flattens meat,
  plunges corers, strikes the egg cassette, rices, presses skin-on produce ("press-peel"), forms patties
  and dumplings, doses pastes from a small syringe tube, and carves boneless roasts.
* **P0, the turning position under the column.** A loose carrier ring with pin teeth lies on three PEEK
  rollers round a 3 kW OEM induction coil (K6's design); the pot's skirt drops into the ring and stands
  2 mm above the glass. P0 turns at 0–500 rpm (20 Nm to 120 rpm). Tools hang from the crosshead through
  the aperture and stand still in the turning pot: scraper (stirring, folding, mash), roller and scraper
  (kneading, creaming, mince mass), spiral guide (rounding), spike (peeling). P0 also spins the salad
  basket, the knurled peeling pot and the plate during plating.
* **The handler.** X (1 400 mm) in a dry rail box at the top rear, a mast hanging from it, Z carriage on
  the mast, a Y arm (380 mm), a wrist with **one roll axis about Y** (0–200 rpm, 12 Nm) and a pin jaw that
  grips the **common tang** (6 × 32 × 60, two Ø 8 holes) on every item, or a GN flange of a storage box.
  Load pins in the jaw weigh everything it holds. It carries, tips boxes (dosing), pours, flips pan and
  rack pairs, spins produce on a fork against the peeler post, rolls dough and Rouladen, holds the stick
  blender, opens the oven door and pulls its rack, places pieces, and moves ware to the rinse seat and port.
* **Bay B:** two plain OEM induction zones H1 (3 kW) and H2 (2.3 kW) on the cell's centre line under one
  glass; a front strip with the seasoning cup on a 300 g cell.
* **Bay C:** the port shelf at deck level (bench B, 10 kg cell, shutter to the transport), the
  rinse-and-dump seat, a bought countertop combi-steam oven turned 90° (mouth towards bay B, door opened
  by the handler), and the tool wall above it.
* **Washing:** none in the cell except the cold rinse seat and the splash-zone nozzles. All ware goes
  through the port to the machine's bought dishwasher (C4 R-10).

What survives of K5: the tube–piston–die principle, gravity into the pot, the force–travel signature of
every stroke, end trimming by first and last cut, paste syringes, loose-floor moulds, patties by stroke.
What is new: the column over the heated turning pot, the knife cage, bought dicer sets, head splitter,
press-peel, rack breading, the stick blender, no in-cell washing.

## 3. How the hard operations work now

Conventions: "event" = one handler pick-carry-place, one tool change, one dose, one pair flip (the unit of
C3 X4 / C5 S5). Confidence H / M / L as in round 1. Times for 4 persons unless stated.

### 3.1 Dosing, eggs (the front end, now without dock and egg module)

* **Boxes** arrive opened at bench B (lid station outside the cell, C4 R-4). The jaw clamps the box's
  short-side GN flange, carries it over the target (tube mouth or pot), rolls it to pour over the far end
  with a ±2° dither at 8 Hz, and stops on the jaw load pins (loss in weight, ±5 g [E, K2 used ±5 g]) or on
  the camera count of pieces at the tube mouth. Seasoning: the handler tips a little into the seasoning cup
  on the 300 g cell (±0.1 g), then tips the cup. Powders: tip with dither, ±5 g; flour for baking ±2 % by
  re-weighing. 2 events per dose. Conf. M (tilt-pouring from boxes is untested for every concept).
* **Pastes and butter**: decanted at first opening into the small tube (K5's syringe: ≤ 1 % residue,
  ±1 mL by stroke). 2 events. H.
* **Eggs**: egg tongs set one egg in the cradle of the egg cassette (standing in the aperture); the foot
  strikes 50–100 N, the cradle spreads (SM-165 kinematics), the contents fall into the slotted saucer
  below; camera checks shell and yolk; the handler lifts the saucer by its tang and tips it into the
  target. Separation by the saucer slot. Beaten egg for mixtures is poured through the 4.5 mm plate (shell
  insurance, K2). 12–15 s per egg, 3 events. M–H (G-assembly 7.2).

### 3.3 Dice an onion (and everything firm)

Peeled onion (#9) tipped from its box into the green tube on the **6 mm push-dicer** cassette; the
bought pusher head goes on top; the foot pushes at ≤ 1.5 kN [E, hand-lever dicers do it]; onion layers
separate into dice of 6 × 6 × layer thickness [K]; the knife cage cuts any long sticks at 6 mm advance.
Dice fall into the pot on P0 — usually the pot in which they will be sweated, with the fat already hot
(the K2 idea). One or two onions per stroke, 8 s per stroke, 2 events per batch (tip, pusher). Potatoes
and carrots: 10 mm set, single layer of 2–3 pieces, sticks cut by the cage at 10 mm; 1 kg in 3 min. Raw
chicken: tempered 20 min in the freezer first (R-02, class a), else it extrudes ragged (K5's finding).
Root-plate bits of bought peeled onions end up in the dice (as with a cook). H for onion, potato, carrot;
M for soft tomato (20 mm wedges instead).

### 3.4 Rouladen: fill, roll, secure, sear, braise (B1, 8 rolls)

1. **Slices** arrive interleaved or singled by the package-opening module (G9 stays a shared open point);
   each lies on a GN 1/2 tray.
2. **Flatten**: tray on the anvil in the aperture, platen on the foot, 1.5 kN for 3 s with a water film
   (class a, like pressing with a mallet's flat). 10 s.
3. **Spread and fill**: the handler holds the tray under the column; the small tube with the slot cap lays
   a 60 × 2 mustard ribbon along the slice and **stops 35 mm before the flap end** (stroke control); the
   flap end is salted and dusted with flour from the sifter cup (seam glue, G-assembly 4.2 (2)). Onion
   (6 mm) and gherkin (6 mm) fall from the green tube in a row across the start end while the tray moves
   in X; bacon is bought diced (R-07 a). 30 s per slice.
4. **Roll**: the slotted mandrel Ø 12 on the roll axis (axis along Y) slides over the start edge; the
   tray has two plough rails that fold the long edges in 15 mm (G-assembly 4.2 (1)); the mandrel rolls
   along the stationary slice with 10 N (K1's method), 2.5 turns; the camera stops the roll with the flap
   at 5 o'clock; the mandrel withdraws in −Y through a U-notch stripper on the comb cradle, leaving the roll
   seam-down in its slot. 40 s per roll.
5. **Secure**: when four rolls lie in the cradle, the **GA-20 raft fork** (two flat tines 6 × 1.5,
   300 long, 60 apart) is pushed in X through the cradle's guide slots and through all four rolls
   (5–10 N each), 10 mm behind the flap edge. Two rafts of four. The cradle is lifted away. R-01 class a.
6. **Sear**: rafts in the braiser (GN 1/2-65) on H1 at 220 °C with fat, seam face first for 90 s, then
   each raft is turned by its handle with the roll axis; about 60 % of the surface browns (GA-20). Rafts out
   onto the tray.
7. **Fond**: meanwhile onion and carrot (6 mm) and 30 g tomato paste (small tube) fall into the 2.5 L
   pot on P0 with fat; roasted 6 min with the scraper; tipped into the braiser; deglazed with wine and
   stock (C1 A-2 sequence). Rafts back in, lid on.
8. **Braise**: oven 160 °C, 90 min (or H1 at simmer).
9. **Gravy**: rafts lifted out to the warm tray; the braiser contents are poured into the green tube on
   the 4.5 mm plate and **pressed through** (passiert, the traditional German gravy) into the 2.5 L pot on
   P0; thickened with starch slurry while P0 turns against the scraper.
10. **Plate**: the stripper comb pushes the rolls off the raft onto the plate, seam down (1–3 N each).

Events ≈ 8 × 4 + 22 = 54 for 8 rolls. Conf. M (H for holding by the raft; M for the mandrel start and
the seam angle).

### 3.5 Breaded Schnitzel in 3–4 mm of fat (B2)

The cutlet is never gripped. It lives on a **wire rack** (Ø 250 frame with a tang, wires Ø 2 at 25 mm
pitch, two cutlets per rack) from the flour to the plate.

1. Flour tray (GN 1/2-20, 8 mm flour bed) on bench B; rack laid in; cutlets placed on the rack by tongs
   from the pack tray; flour sifted on top; the **platen flattens** them to 5 mm right in the flour tray
   (tray on the anvil): flour stops sticking to the platen.
2. Rack lifted, dithered (excess flour off), lowered into the egg tray (GN 1/4, 150 mL beaten egg,
   3.5 mm deep) on H2 (cold); 20 mL ladled over the top; drained 5 s.
3. Rack lowered into the crumb tray (15 mm crumb bed) on P0 (cold): the crumbs carry the cutlet, so the
   underside is crumbed between the wires; crumbs sifted on top; pressed with the turner at 20 N; lifted.
   Coverage ≥ 95 % expected; faint wire lines on one face [E].
4. **Fry**: the rack goes into the 28 cm pan on H1 with **200 mL clarified butter or lard at 170 °C
   (3.8 mm)**; the handler rocks the rack ±10 mm at 1 Hz so that fat washes over the edges (the crust
   puffs) and ladles fat over the top twice. 2.5 min.
5. **Turn**: the second rack is laid on top, tangs back to back; the pair is lifted 30 mm, drained 5 s,
   rolled 180° about its own centre (coordinated X–Z wrist path) **over the pan**, lowered (GA-36). 2.5 min.
6. **Out**: lifted, drained, tilted 25° over the plate; the cutlets slide off against the turner.

Two cutlets per batch, two batches for 4; the planner fries them **last** (C1 rule 2), batch 1 waits ≤ 6
min in the oven at 70 °C, vented. Breading ≤ 10 min before frying. Events ≈ 22 for 4. Conf. M (rack
breading is new; GA-36 is M–H). Fat afterwards: the pan is tipped into a fat can at the dump seat when
cool.

### 3.6 Frikadellen (B3)

Onion (6 mm) falls into the 5 L pot on P0; mince (box tip), soaked roll, egg (cassette), mustard (small
tube), salt and pepper (cup). The **kneading set** on the crosshead mixes for 90 s at 60 rpm. Portioning:
a bought **portion scoop** (disher, lever squeezed by the jaw) takes 90 g balls (±5 % on the load pins)
and sets them into the oiled 28 cm pan standing on P0; P0 indexes 45° between drops (polar placement, K4),
eight per pan; the turner presses each to 20 mm — the hand-formed, pan-smashed shape that C1 rates most
traditional. Pan to H1, 4 min, pan-pair flip (≤ 30 mL fat, R-03), 4 min, probe to 72 °C (wireless).
Events ≈ 30. Conf. M–H.

The column's former (Ø 55, by stroke) stays for masses that are already in the tube: Klöße from riced
potato, gnocchi, rolls, falafel; pieces are rounded by the **spiral guide** on P0 (the bakery rounder:
turning bowl, fixed spiral track [K]) — M.

### 3.7 Mash (B3)

Potatoes boiled in their skins in the basket on H1 (GP-P1); basket lifted, drained 20 s, tipped into the
green tube on the **4.5 mm plate**; the press rices them at 1–2 kN straight into the 5 L pot on P0 that
holds 150 mL hot milk and butter; skins stay on the plate (C1's "best ricer mash"). The scraper folds at
30 rpm for 40 s. 1 kg in 2 loads, 90 s. Events 6. H.

### 3.8 Kneading and shaping dough (B5)

Flour, salt, yeast (box tips and cup) and 30 °C water (spout, flow meter) into the 5 L pot on P0; the
**roller-and-scraper set** hangs from the crosshead; P0 at 60–100 rpm for 8 min (Ankarsrum [K]; 1.6 kg at
14–20 Nm, within 20 Nm). Proof in the oven at 30 °C, lidded, 45 min. Shaping:

* **Pizza** (R-05: tray or round both class a): the dough is cut into portions by the wire cage (dough
  pushed through the open mouth, 2 × 410 g or 4 × 205 g by stroke) straight onto a floured Ø 26 pizza pan
  standing on P0; the handler **rolls it with the adjustable pin (4 mm rings)** in radial passes while P0
  indexes 30° between passes — a round, rolled base; two rests of 60 s for spring-back. Sauce ladled in a
  spiral while P0 turns; mozzarella diced (10 mm) or grated (rasp cage) falls evenly on the turning pan;
  salami placed by tongs in a polar pattern. Oven 250 °C, 10–12 min, two pans per load. M.
* **Flammkuchen** 2 mm: same with the 2 mm rings, quark-based dough, at the pin's limit for stiff dough
  (M–L).
* **Loaf**: the whole ball into the loose-floor tin. **Rolls**: portions through the Ø 55 former, wire
  cut, rounded by the spiral guide. **Wrappers** (gyoza, Maultaschen, samosa, turnovers, mandated class
  c): 8–20 g balls pressed one by one between the floured anvil and platen to Ø 90 × 1 mm at 2–3 kN [C:
  squeeze flow, yield stress 20 kPa], filled by the small tube, folded and crimped in a bought hinged
  dumpling mould whose lid the roll axis turns over. L–M, counted only in the high coverage figure.

### 3.9 Pancake flip (B8)

Batter blended with the stick blender in the 2.5 L pot. Pan A (28 cm, skirt) on P0 at 190 °C, 3 g fat;
ladle 95 mL, P0 spins 90 rpm for 3 s to spread (SM-097). After 50 s: release check (P0 jerks 5°, camera
sees the pancake slide, G-assembly rule 4); pan B (preheated on H1, matched rim with drip skirt) is set
upside down on A, tangs back to back; the pair is lifted, rolled 180° about its centre over P0, set on
H1; pan A is lifted off and back on P0 for the next pancake. Pancake finishes in B; B tipped onto the warm
stack plate. 95 s per pancake with two pans. Events 4 per pancake. M (G-assembly 7.1 rules).

### 3.10 Draining pasta (B4)

400 g spaghetti in the lift-out basket in the 5 L pot with 3.5 L water on H1 (COK-016: ≥ 3 L). Lift basket,
drain 20 s over the pot, tip into the sauce pan or the serving dish. Pot water: after the meal the pot
(5.5 kg) is tipped slowly at the dump seat (within the 6 kg payload; R7 is about the full 9 L pot, which
K5b does not have). Events 3. H.

### 3.11 Carving (CAR)

Boneless roast rested 10 min, cross-section ≤ 100 × 100 mm (most roasts for 2–4): stood on end in the
green tube on the open mouth, the foot's spiked pad holds it at 30–50 N; the sickle cage cuts one slice per
revolution at 30 rpm, advance 3–8 mm; slices fall onto the warm platter or plate inside the cage on P0,
which turns with the cage — the slices land **fanned in a circle**. 60 s per kg. Braised, tender roasts:
chill, slice, reheat (R-02, class a). Larger roasts (6-person, > 100 mm): halved lengthwise by the
splitter's two-blade insert first (M). Bone-in poultry: parts (R-06 b). M–H for firm roasts.

### 3.12 Plating 2–4 portions

Plates (warmed in the oven) on the plate carrier on P0. Components in sectors: P0 turns the plate by 120°
between components. Mash: portion scoop gives a neat mound (or the column pipes it through the slot cap of
the small tube while P0 turns: a rosette). Meat: peel/turner places the piece; Rouladen stripped off the
raft; roast slices fanned by the cage. Vegetables: slotted ladle. Sauce: ladle beside the meat, never over
the crust. Garnish: herbs cut by the cage (small tube, 1.5 mm chiffonade) fall onto the plate turning under
the die. 60–80 s per plate, 3–5 events each; plates leave through the port to the heated hand-over shelf
(C4 R-11). A course for 4 is out within 5 min of the first plate; for 2 within 3 min. M.

## 5. Cleaning

### 5.1 Principle

1. **Everything that touches food is ware** (C2 R-1): tubes, pistons, cassettes, knife cage, crosshead
   tools, vessels, racks, stem tools. Each is emptied (the piston pigs the tube to ≤ 5 g [E:C], the pusher
   clears the grid), **cold-rinsed at the rinse seat within 2 minutes** (upward fan nozzle, 0.3 L, 5 s,
   C2 R-7), parked on bench B and sent through the port to the machine's dishwasher, hygiene programme
   ≥ 70 °C for 10 min (A0 ≈ 60, C2 R-3, verified there with a logger and a per-item camera, C2 R-11).
2. **No wash system in the cell**: no boiler, sump, pump, hood or parts basket. The only fixed fluid
   parts are the rinse-seat nozzle, the spouts at P0 and H1, and the splash nozzles (mains hot water with
   a venturi detergent dose).
3. **Fixed surfaces are splash zone only**, with one exception named below (the die-shelf ledge), and
   are sprayed after every cooked meal (short) and washed once a day (full), then dried by the hood fan
   (HYG-045).
4. **Nothing above an open vessel** except the plain crosshead plate and the arm when it reaches over: no
   drive, no rod collar, no parked tool (C2 R-2). The press seals are below food level.

### 5.3 Raw meat and ready-to-eat

* **Separate instances**: red tube and piston, red tongs, red knife-cage bridge; the disher, racks and
  breading trays are used for raw meat only once per meal and go straight to the rinse seat and port.
* **Sequence rule** (as K5, kept): produce and RTE first, raw animal food last at the column; a cassette
  that touched raw meat leaves the cell at once and is not reused in the meal.
* **The column's fixed parts never touch food**: the shelf ledge is rinsed after a raw-meat tube has stood
  on it; the foot touches only the piston's back.
* **Unwashed produce is class R** (C2 R-5): it is tipped only into the wash basket in the wash pot on P0
  or onto the fork; both go to the rinse seat before the washed produce goes on. The wash water is tipped
  at the dump seat, through the strainer, never over a vessel.
* Jaw pins: rinsed after gripping any raw-meat tray (the tang has a collar; the jaw never touches food).

   plates for plating (K2's K/H made simpler with K6's loose ring).
2. **Pull-down closed press with a Ø 260 cassette aperture and bought push-dicer sets.** 4 kN, two rods
   diagonal to the die, drive under the deck: nothing above the food, no C-frame, cheap blades. The large
   aperture lets the same press split whole heads with a centre corer, flatten meat, plunge corers, strike
   the egg cassette and carve.
3. **Press-peel — "skin and stone stay, flesh passes".** A ricer plate or a dicer grid under a press peels
   by passing: banana for baking, avocado for guacamole, mango cheeks and avocado halves to cubes, ginger,
   garlic, boiled potato, roasted pumpkin, pared citrus to juice. It turns a generic-peeling class of
   #25 into zero extra hardware and recovers two meals nobody else could make.
4. **Rack breading with the rack-pair turn.** The cutlet lies on a wire rack from the flour to the plate:
   floured, egged, crumbed (the crumb bed carries it, so the underside is coated), flattened in its flour
   tray, fried in 200 mL of fat and turned as a pair over the pan. No grip on the crust, real
   shallow-frying, no pan inversion with fat in the joint.
5. **Knife cage on the turning ring.** A loose ring with a blade bridge stands on the turntable around the
   pot: one revolution is one face cut, so a press needs no cut-off motor; slices and roast slices land
   fanned on a turning plate. With the turntable it also gives polar placement for patties, toppings and
   plating.
