# K8b — Sealed tub with magnetic pucks, improved

Round P5a improvement of K8 (`concepts/K8-sealed-tub-magnetic-puck.md`). Inputs: the K8 document, the K8
findings and round-2 recommendations of C1–C5, the decision matrix, sections 1, 2, 4, 11 and 12 of K1–K6,
the sub-mechanism inventory of the catalogue, the gap documents G-produce and G-assembly-meat, DECISIONS
#1–#28. Nothing was built. Tags as in K8: **[C]** calculated here, **[E]** estimate, **[K]** known
practice, **[U]** unverified, **[S]** from a source document.

**Core idea kept:** one closed stainless tub; nothing moves through any wall; two passive pucks inside are
dragged and turned by magnet heads behind the front wall; the tub washes itself. **What changed most:**
the pucks only position and hold (≤ 40 N, rated at half the model magnet figures); force and speed come
from one flush canned floor hub; hard produce is cut by turning it against a held blade (the "T-lathe");
the hob is a bought household flex hob; ware is washed during the meal in a downdraft well; the 40 kg drive
door drops into the plinth.

## 2. The improved concept

### 2.1 Definition in one page

One welded stainless tub, **1130 × 500 × 700 mm inside**, floor at 900 mm room height, in a module **1180
wide × 600 deep**. Nothing moves through any wall. The front wall is a 90 mm drive cassette (door) whose inner
1.5 mm skin of 1.4404 is the **drive wall**: behind it two X–Z gantries move two magnet heads; inside, two
passive **pucks** cling to the skin, are dragged in X and Z and turn their rotor about Y (the roll axis).
Three columns stand on the floor:

* **W, wash column (X 0–200)**: the **downdraft well** in the floor (mouth 180 × 340, 450 deep; a bowl
  Ø 280 × 180 or a GN 1/2 turned on edge fits), the **clean-air tool nook** above it on the left wall, the
  **hatch** in the left wall at floor level, two park pegs for the pucks.
* **P, prep column (X 200–530)**: the **recessed TS hub** in the floor (T: 30 Nm, 0–500 rpm; S coaxial
  inside it: 1.5 Nm, 0–6000 rpm), the dosing port above it, a steel floor. Everything that needs force,
  speed or a second horizontal axis happens on T: board, bowl, jug, press cup, spit, plate.
* **H, hob column (X 534–1126)**: a **bought 60 cm household induction hob with two flex zones**,
  under-mounted as the floor (glass 592 × 522), touch board replaced by an interface. Three cooking
  positions: GN 1/2 on flex zone L; flex zone R either one GN 1/2 or a GN 1/4 (front half) plus a Ø 200
  pot (rear half).

A strip 60 mm deep along the drive wall (the **fall lane**) never holds a vessel. The oven stands outside
the module and is served by the transport system. The tub washes all preparation and cooking ware itself,
during the meal in the well and at the end with a 10 min chamber wash.

### 2.3 Pucks, heads and the R50 rating

**Head**: ring Ø 150 of 20 blocks 14 × 14 × 6 N42SH on 6 mm iron, rotated by the ring-spin motor; four fixed
orientation magnets; three Hall sensors (lag in X, Z and angle). The head rides on **three UHMW-PE glide pads**
on the dry face of the skin (610 N over 3 × 700 mm² = 0.3 MPa: no Hertz dents, C3 K8-3); the gap is set by the
skin, 4.0 mm magnet to magnet. **No retract drive**: travel happens at full clamp, release is done by hanging
the puck's bail on a peg and moving the head 200 mm down; the peg holds the ≤ 280 N breakaway force, the
alternating poles then push the puck off the wall and it hangs free. Re-acquisition is the reverse.

**Puck** (3: two working, one spare): rotor = 20 blocks 14 × 14 × 5 N42SH (150 °C grade, cheaper than SmCo) in
a fully welded 1.4404 can; spider with four PEEK skids (through-riveted, dated wear parts, no blind seat) and
four orientation magnets; a **one-piece moulded silicone bumper ring**; bayonet spigot Ø 25 with three lugs;
bail on top; 1.1 kg. Rotor against spider is the **pincer** (closes the one-piece U-tongs, the disher sweep,
the egg cracker).

**Ratings.** K8's surface-charge model gave a ring clamp of 638 N at 4 mm; C3's independent magnetic-circuit
estimate gave about 500 N. K8b plans every task with **R50 = 50 % of the model** (64 % of C3's figure):

| Quantity | Model (K8 App. A) | R50 (planning) | If the rig shows ≥ 80 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clamp N, ring + spider | 768 N | 384 N | 610 N |
| Shear at 4 mm lag | 273 N | 137 N | 220 N |
| Skid friction µ 0.15–0.25 (wet wall 0.05–0.1) | — | 58–96 N (19–38 N) | 90–150 N |
| **In-plane force while moving** | — | **≥ 40 N** worst case dry; ≥ 100 N wet wall | 110 N |
| Tipping moment (N × 62 mm); rated half | 48 Nm | 24 Nm; **rated 12 Nm** | 20 Nm |
| **Rotor torque**, rated | 20 Nm at 3.7° | **6 Nm** | 12 Nm |
| Pull-off (force away from the wall) | — | 190 N | 250 N |
| Push towards the wall | — | unlimited (skids) | unlimited |

Every puck task in sections 3–4 stays inside the R50 column. Physics that makes this possible: forces that
push **towards** the wall cost nothing, so tools are arranged so that the reaction of the work (T-lathe
cutting, kneading, scraping) points into the wall (3.1).

**Fall lane.** A puck that loses its head falls along the skin (it leaves with almost no horizontal speed)
into the strip Y 0–60, where no vessel stands. In W and P the strip is a coved 3 mm steel trough sloping to
the well; in H it is the 70 mm glass margin in front of the coils. The puck works at most 250 mm above the
glass; with the bumper a fall is 2.7 J on a blunt silicone ring, like a pan dropped from 25 cm [E]. Its
utensil may tip into the vessel it was working in: it is clean ware and the food is kept unless the camera
sees damage. Recovery: the other puck lifts the fallen one by its bail and holds it to the wall until the
head snaps in; the spare covers meanwhile.

### 2.4 The drive door

The door cassette (skin, X rails, two bridges, two Z carriages, two heads, insulated décor panel; about 40 kg)
runs on four rollers in two vertical guides in the plinth. **To open, it slides 700 mm straight down into a
pocket at the front of the plinth** (room height 200–900), balanced by two constant-force spring motors: hand
force below 50 N, no projection into the room (PHY-010), no height above 1600 (PHY-002). In its last 20 mm
of upward travel the rollers run onto ramps that move it 8 mm towards the tub and compress the gasket against
a metal stop (lift-and-slide principle [K]). Before the lock releases, both pucks hang on their pegs and the
tools are in the nook (idea 1). Drives and sensors stay in the door, **electronics sit in the plinth** (cable
loop in the pocket); a fan draws room air up through the cassette (C3 K8-2). Service of any drive: décor
panel off, door closed, from the room (as in K8).

## 3. How the hard operations work now

### 3.1 Four principles that replace force and the missing axis

1. **Polar work on T.** The puck places a tool at any X and any height; T turns the work by any angle (±0.1°,
   ±0.26 mm at r = 150). Together they reach every point of a Ø 300 disc: board, bowl, plate, pizza tray.
   This is K4's polar placement on K8's floor drive. The hob needs no Y motion because GN ware lies with its
   long side along Y and the paddle, turner and fork span Y.
2. **T-lathe: the food turns, the tool is held.** Produce sits on a spit on T (a corkscrew driven into the
   stem end by T itself while the toothed tailstock or the tongs keep the piece from turning; no push is
   needed). A blade held by a puck meets it. The **cutting force is tangential and comes from T** (30 Nm =
   300 N at r = 100 [C]); the puck supplies only the feed (5–20 N). The blade is set on the +X side of the
   piece and T turns so that the surface there moves towards the wall: the cutting reaction pushes the tool
   **into** the wall, which the skids carry without limit. Operations: peel (shoe A, 1.5 mm), pare (shoe B,
   5 mm), zest and grate (rasp), slit (shoe A, 2.5 mm), **part** (a wide thin blade plunged radially: a
   horizontal slice falls free on the blade, which then carries it like a cake server), **trepan** (the
   knife point plunged downwards at a fixed radius: a core column is cut free around the spit axis).
3. **Press cup for compression.** Tube Ø 110 on T; T turns an Rd 28 × 6 screw in the base, a follower with
   a PE piston rises, food is pushed up through the die on top. T = F × 4.3 mm [S, K8 2.2], so 13 Nm gives
   3 kN at 15 mm/s; the force loop closes inside the cup; the reaction torque (13 Nm) goes through a 200 mm
   arm against the rear wall (65 N). Product is swept off the die face by the wire bow into the spout
   collar, which drops it into a vessel on the neighbouring hob zone or into a cup. Dies: bought push-dicer
   grids 10 and 15 mm, ricer 3 mm, garlic insert, orifice Ø 50, flat lid.
4. **Reactions into the wall.** Kneading, scraping and lathe work are arranged so that the reaction is
   normal to the drive wall; the puck only positions (≤ 40 N in plane, R50).

### 3.3 Dice an onion

Peeled onion (bought or GP-11) is halved by a push cut on the board (20–40 N; the wet wall raises the
allowance to ≥ 100 N). Two halves go cut face down into the press cup, grid 10 on top; T drives the piston up
3 kN max; every 10 mm the wire bow sweeps the die face, the onion's own layers give the third cut. Dice fall
through the spout into the pan on flex zone L. One onion in 40 s; 1 kg of vegetables in 3 min. Fine dice for
one person: the cook's method on the board (vertical cuts in X steps, T turns 90°, horizontal cuts by
parting with the board turning under a horizontal blade). Confidence H (grid), M (knife).

### 3.4 Rouladen (fill, roll, secure, sear, braise)

1. **Fill.** Board on T carries the red silicone mat; the slice is laid along X by the U-tongs. Mustard from
   the 0.15 L cup, spread by the paddle **stopping 35 mm before the flap**; flap salted and floured from the
   dock (C1 10.3 rule 4). Bacon, onion strips and gherkin laid by the tongs; T turns the board, so every
   place on the slice is reachable.
2. **Roll.** Long edges folded in 15 mm by the paddle edge (G-assembly 4.2 rule 1). P2 lifts the hem bar and
   carries it over in an X–Z arc (the roll axis is Y, which is the puck's natural plane); 2.5 turns; the
   camera stops when the measured slice length is wound, flap at 5 o'clock.
3. **Secure.** Rolls go seam-down into the comb cradle, four side by side. **GA-20 raft**: a two-tine fork
   whose handle is bent to point at the wall (a T shape) is pushed along X through the cradle slots and all
   four rolls (4 × 5–10 N [S]). The cradle is lifted away; the raft is a rigid set with a handle outside the
   food. 8 Rouladen = 2 rafts.
4. **Sear.** GN 1/2-65 on flex zone L at 200 °C; raft seam side down 90 s untouched; then **rolled 180°
   about its own handle axis** (the T-handle lies on the puck axis, so the raft turns in place; 1.5 kg at
   < 20 mm offset: < 0.5 Nm); second face 3 min; rafts parked on a warm lid. Onion and tomato paste roasted
   in the fond 5 min, deglazed with wine (A-2).
5. **Braise.** Rafts back in, stock to half height, lid on, **on the hob** at about 0.3 kW for 90 min (the
   oven is not needed). Lifted out by the handle, drained 10 s, rolls pushed off by the stripper comb onto
   the plates on T. Gravy thickened in the pan.

Time: 90 s per roll for filling and rolling, 8 rolls 12 min. Confidence: M–H for holding (raft), M for
rolling. Fallback: single pins (SM-085) counted back by the camera.

### 3.5 Breaded Schnitzel in 3–4 mm of fat

Cutlets bought thin (DEC-8 permits butcher cuts) or flattened between the folded silicone mat by the rolling
pin (ENVELOPE, idea 18). **Breading**: the 3-sector dish (flour, egg, crumbs) on T; the U-tongs hold a cutlet
by its edge; T turns 120° from sector to sector; the turner presses crumbs (20 N), turns the cutlet by a 180°
roll, presses again. 40 s per cutlet, breading ≤ 10 min before frying. **Frying**: GN 1/2-40 on flex zone L
with **300 mL of fat = 3.5 mm** [C: 300 mL / 860 cm²]. Two cutlets lie on the lower **lift rack** in the fat;
the cup on the roll axis bastes fat over the top so the crust puffs ("souffliert"); after 2.5 min the upper
rack is laid on, the pair is lifted (1.1 kg), drained 5 s over the pan, rolled 180°, lowered (GA-36). Drips
fall back into the pan; nothing of the hot fat is inverted. Four cutlets = 2 batches of 5 min; the first
batch waits on a rack over a warm empty GN on zone R (vented, ≤ 6 min, C1 A-4). Fat afterwards poured into a
lidded cup and out through the hatch. Confidence M–H.

### 3.6 Frikadellen

Soaked roll squeezed in the bowl by the paddle against the wall; mince from the hatch tray tipped in; egg,
onion dice, mustard, salt, parsley; **kneading roller arm** 90 s at 60 rpm with the bowl on T (reaction into
the wall). Portions by the **disher** (85 g ±5 g, sweep band turned by the pincer), dropped back into the
turning bowl and **rounded**: a curved paddle held still at the bowl wall makes the ball roll (bakery
rounding, SM-073), 8 s per ball. Balls set into the hot GN 1/2-40 (2 mm fat) and pressed to 20 mm by the
turner (15–25 N). Turned singly with the low turner, 6 min per side; core probe 72 °C. 8 pieces in 25 min.
No cylinders (R-10 respected). Confidence M.

### 3.7 Mash

Potatoes (1 kg) boiled in their skin in the GN 1/2-100 basket on zone L, 22 min. Basket lifted, drained 20 s;
press cup with the ricer die stands on T, spout over the GN 1/4-65 with hot milk and butter on zone R. Two
loads of 500 g, 3 kN max, the skins stay below the die as a cake; the paddle folds in milk and butter.
4 min. Confidence H.

### 3.8 Kneading and shaping dough

**Knead**: bowl Ø 280 on T at 60–100 rpm; the kneading roller arm stands with its foot on the drive wall and
its roller 70 mm from the bowl axis on the X line through T, so the dough drags it towards the wall
(Ankarsrum principle [K]); P1 only holds the arm in X and Z. 1 kg of flour 8 min at about 8 Nm, 1.6 kg at
14–20 Nm (T 30 Nm). **Shape**: dough tipped onto the floured board on T and cut by weight with the knife;
**rolls** are rounded in the turning bowl like the Frikadellen; **pizza** is rolled round on the Ø 300 tray
on T: the rigid pin lies radially and the puck presses 30–40 N while T turns (a potter's jigger, SM-105), the
dough grows to Ø 280 in 6–8 turns; sauce poured in a spiral while T turns; toppings placed in polar
co-ordinates. Sticky or rich dough is rolled under the folded mat (ENVELOPE). Confidence M.

### 3.9 Pancake flip

Batter mixed in the jug on S (30 s). Crêpe pan A (Ø 26, ground rim) on flex zone L, 3 g of butter; 100 mL
batter from the cup while the puck tilts the pan by its tang (spread by roll ± 15°); 90 s. Pan B, hot and
buttered on zone R, is set face down on A; the vessel fork clamps both tangs; roll 180° (≤ 30 mL free fat,
G-assembly 7.1 rules); A is lifted off. Release check first: a 5 mm jerk, the camera must see the pancake
move. 8 pancakes in 14 min. Confidence M–H (C1 R-03 counts it as a traditional result).

### 3.10 Draining pasta

GN 1/2-100 with its lift-out basket on zone L, 4 L water. The vessel fork lifts the basket (1.5 kg at
200 mm: 3 Nm tilt), holds it 20 s, rolls it to tip the pasta into the sauce pan. The water stays in the pot;
when zone L is free the pot slides along the floor to the well and is **pivot-poured**: its −X bottom edge
rests on the well rim and the puck lifts only the far end (half the weight, 27 N in Z). Confidence H.

### 3.11 Carving

Boneless roast stands on end on the corkscrew spit (bottom 40 mm is the grip and is used for the sauce). The
wide parting blade takes **slices from the top across the grain**: radial plunge while T turns at 20 rpm,
cutting force from T (cooked meat 25–50 N at r ≤ 60 mm: < 3 Nm), feed by the puck 5–10 N. Each slice lies
on the blade and is laid, overlapping, on a warm GN 1/4 on zone R (the plate cannot be on T while the
roast is).
8 mm slices, 7 s each. Confidence M for firm roasts, L–M for fork-tender braises (they may twist on the
spit): fallback push cut on the board with the long knife while T oscillates ±2° (a draw of ±4 mm along the
blade at r = 120 mm), or chill-slice-reheat. Bone-in poultry: cooked as parts (R-06 b).

### 3.12 Plating 2–4 portions nicely

Four warm plates come in on the pass tray (two per tray). One plate at a time on the plate carrier on T:
meat by the stripper comb or the parting blade (fanned by T), vegetables by roll-pouring the 0.5 L cup at a
set radius, mash or rice as a dome from the disher, sauce poured as an arc while T turns, garnish by tongs.
40 s per plate [E]; 4 plates in 2.7 min (SRV-009: 3 min); plates leave two at a time on the pass tray. The
camera checks the rim for drips; a squeegee-wipe of the rim is one pass of the paddle edge. Confidence M.

## 5. Cleaning

### 5.1 During the meal: the downdraft well

Every item is washed as soon as it is free (HYG-030: ≤ 60 min; usually ≤ 10 min). The puck lowers it on edge
into the well on the vessel fork (a 150 mm drop link keeps the puck itself above the floor); the item's top
edge is ≥ 40 mm below the floor. Cycle:

| Step | What | Time | Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wash | 8 jets, 60 °C alkaline-enzyme liquor recirculated from the 8 L tank through a sediment trap; item turned 180° by the roll halfway | 20 s | 0 (tank) |
| Rinse | fresh water 85 °C, 6 L/min | 8 s | 0.8 L |
| **Sanitise (class R items and anything for RTE after raw work)** | 85 °C water recirculated from the boiler through the well for **60 s** (surface ≥ 82 °C after about 5 s on thin steel; A0 ≈ 100–190 [C]), return temperature logged; then 5 s fresh | 65 s | 4 L per session (several items), reused as the chamber pre-rinse |
| Inspect | item turned under the ceiling camera (white, UV-A) | 5 s | — |

**Containment next to open food** (the reason K8 could not wash during the meal, C2 K8-4): the jets act
40–400 mm below the floor and point downwards; the exhaust branch at the bottom of the well draws 100 m³/h,
i.e. **0.45 m/s down through the mouth**, more than the face velocity of a fume cupboard [K: 0.3–0.5 m/s];
fine mist cannot rise against it and ballistic droplets leave a 450 mm deep shaft only near the vertical.
Rule: pans within 300 mm (zone L at X ≥ 534 is 340 mm away) are lidded while the jets run. This is risk 2.

The well also serves as the **produce sink** (GP-W1 baths, 15 L each, sediment to the strainer; unwashed
produce is class R, R-5: the first bath is followed by a hot flush of the well), as the **cooling bath** for
boiled potatoes and blanched tomatoes, and as the **waste port**: all peel, scraps and the strainer content
of the board go to the 2 mm strainer basket, which a puck lifts out into the lidded chip box on the pass
tray. The strainer and the chip box are outside the recirculation loop (R-8).

### 5.2 Clean-air tool nook

The eight working tools hang in the nook, open only towards the drive wall. A fan in the plinth pushes room
air through an F7 pre-filter and an H13 filter, warmed to 45 °C, into the nook (20 m³/h): it leaves the open
face at 0.3 m/s and flows down into the well's downdraft. The nook is thus **upstream of everything**: air runs
nook → work zone → hob exhaust and well. A tool enters the nook only after the well cycle (with the
sanitising step if it touched class R food or will touch RTE food after raw work); it arrives at about 80 °C
and is dry in about 1 min in the warm air. This replaces "clean ware stored in the soiled tub" by "clean ware
stored in a positively ventilated nook inside the tub" (R-6 asks for a closed shutter or a space outside the
air in which raw food is open; the air barrier is argued, not a shutter: risk 2 tests it with settle plates
in the nook). Per-meal ware arrives from the machine's ware store just in time and leaves washed.

### 5.3 After the meal: the chamber wash (10 min) and drying

1. **Scrape** (1 min): paddle and squeegee push floor debris along the fall lane into the well.
2. **Pre-rinse** (1.5 min): the sanitising-loop water and the tank liquor through the two nozzle bars (ceiling
   front edge aiming back and down; rear wall top aiming forward along the ceiling at a grazing angle): rear
   wall, side walls, ceiling, floor, hob glass, the empty TS pocket (plug lifted, it went through the well).
3. **Wash** (4 min): tank liquor at 60 °C recirculated; the **drip bar** runs a falling film of the same liquor
   down the whole drive wall (Γ ≈ 0.1 kg/(m·s), the minimum wetting rate of a falling film [K], 6.6 L/min
   recirculated); **P1 squeegees the drive wall** top to bottom twice in overlapping 300 mm lanes, P2 parks;
   then they swap. No jet hits the drive wall, so nothing drums on the room-facing skin (C3 K8-5).
4. **Pucks** (1 min): hung on their pegs above the well; two side jets in the left wall play along the drive
   wall plane (grazing) over rotor and spider, which hang 5 mm apart.
5. **Rinse** (1.5 min): fresh 85 °C through the nozzle bars and the drip bar.
6. **Dry** (8 min, the tub is usable after 2): exhaust 100 m³/h through the condenser; P1 squeegees the drive
   wall and the floor; the paddle edge clears the hob glass; walls dry from their own heat.

**Next meal**: preparation can start **12 min after the last hand-over** (K8: 25–35 min); powders at the port
after 20 min (dry tub). The day's liquor washes the tub before it is dumped (K6 idea); no liquor is held
between 25 and 55 °C (R-7): the tank stays at 60 °C during the meal and is dumped after the chamber wash.

### 5.6 Raw and ready-to-eat

Order within a meal: dry before wet, **RTE before raw**, raw animal food last in column P (C1 10.3, SM-208).
Red and green boards, two U-tongs; everything that touched class R food is sanitised (60 s at ≥ 82 °C)
before it is used again or parked. After raw work: hot falling film on the W/P segment of the drive wall
(60 s), floor of P flushed by the bar segment and squeegeed into the well, T plug and board carrier through
the well. If RTE work must follow raw work (B1), it is done in vessels or on the freshly sanitised green
board only. Air runs from the nook and the dosing port towards the hob exhaust and the well. One chamber
remains **temporal separation** with disinfection, which is what C5 Q8 asks the customer to accept.

### 5.7 Verification

Every item leaving the well is turned under the ceiling camera (white and UV-A) and compared with its
reference; return temperature and time of every sanitising step are logged (R-3); turbidity of the well
return; one witness coupon on the red board carrier; weekly riboflavin self-test of well, nook and drive wall
(SM-207); a per-meal camera pass over the fixed surfaces after the chamber wash (R-11). A part that fails
is washed again; after a second failure it leaves through the hatch quarantined.

## 8. Best ideas for the combined machine (K9b)

1. **Polar work on a canned turntable.** Any manipulator with X, Z and one roll axis gets full planar
   coverage of a Ø 300 disc from the turntable angle; board, bowl, plate and pizza tray need no Y axis.
   Free with a turntable that the machine needs anyway.
2. **T-lathe: turn the food against a held blade.** Peeling, paring, zesting, grating, parting slices,
   trepanning cores, halving pumpkins and shredding whole cabbages, with the cutting force (300 N) coming from
   the floor drive and only 5–20 N of feed from the manipulator. One spit, three blades and a rasp cover most
   of PRP-039; it makes the manipulator light.
3. **In-meal downdraft wash well with a 60 s hot sanitising loop and a clean-air tool nook above it.** Ware
   is washed beside open food without spraying it (0.45 m/s downdraft), class R items are disinfected inside
   the meal, the same shaft is the produce sink and the waste port, and the cell is free 12 min after a meal.
4. **Recessed coaxial canned hub.** Slow drive (30 Nm) and fast drive (6000 rpm) in one drained floor pocket,
   plug flush with the floor: zero seals, vessels slide over it, one spot kneads, presses, spins, blends and
   turns the lathe.
5. **Household flex hob under-mounted as a tub floor, GN ware on matched oval coils, plus a 60 mm fall lane
   and a drop-down pocket door.** Bought appliance as is (DEC-28), no corner scorching, nothing falls into
   food, and a full-front door that never projects into the room.
