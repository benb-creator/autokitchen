# C1 — Critique of the preparation concepts K1…K8: meal coverage and food result

Round P4, critic C1. View: chef and food technologist. Yardsticks: BRIEF (≥ 95 % of traditional meals; the
named meals), DECISIONS 6–14 (above all #8: the machine washes, peels and cuts all fruit and vegetables
itself, only cut or portioned products such as fish fillet and mince may be bought, no industrial
semi-finished food; #9: peeled onions are the baseline; #10: 1–6 persons; #14: tacos and wraps may be served
as components), requirements section 5 (MEAL-001…021, UO-xx, rulings 5.5), the corpus
`research/02-meal-corpus.md` (248 rows), the two gap documents and the catalogue.

All concept documents are paper estimates by their own advocates. So is this critique: nothing here was
cooked or tested. Markers: **[D]** taken from the concept document (section given); **[C]** recomputed by me
from the corpus rows; **[E]** my estimate as a cook. Severity: **fatal** = the concept, as a whole kitchen,
cannot reach the coverage or the traditional result without giving up a defining element; **major** = a
named meal, a staple (weight 3) or a mandatory criterion of MEAL-015 (doneness, core temperature,
consistency, browning) is missed as designed; **minor** = quality loss with a known remedy. "Fixable" = within
the concept as defined.

Scope: I read sections 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8 of all eight concepts in full, the rest selectively; the two gap
documents in full where they touch food; requirements section 5 in full; every corpus row.

the concept has no fallback.

**Five headline findings.**

1. **Under DECISIONS #8 no concept reaches 95 %, and none can.** Nine meals fall out for every concept even
   with every gap module fitted (IT17 tortellini — weight 3 —, DM33 Maultaschen, CK12 strudel, CK17
   Apfeltaschen: ready dough is now forbidden and X-06 excludes making it; MX06 guacamole and CK16 banana
   bread: nobody peels a banana or pits an avocado; AS08, AS09, IN07: folded and pleated wrappers). With the
   8 exclusions of 5.4 the ceiling is **231 of 248 = 93.1 %** [C]. MEAL-003 (all staples) fails for everyone
   on IT17 alone, and MEAL-004 fails for everyone in two categories (cakes 12/17 = 71 %, Asian 9/12 = 75 %)
   [C]. This needs a customer ruling, not a better concept (section 10.1).
2. **The eight explorers used eight standards.** Their claims (88.7 % to 96 %) are not comparable: they
   bought different things, counted "served as components" differently, mixed the 19 mandated adaptations
   into their own budget, and three of them simply did not list meals they cannot make (section 2.2). K6's
   95–96 %, the only claim at the target, rests on an operation-level count that omits the folded-pastry
   family and cordon bleu.
3. **Three concepts silently assumed peeled garlic** (K3, K4, K8), five bought pepper strips or frozen cut
   beans (K2, K3, K5, K6, K7), two bought cabbage already cut (K5, K8), and one bought apples cored (K3).
   Under #8 each of these turns into a class (c) meal or a lost meal. Most are repaired by one small
   concept-independent module from G-produce — which is why K3 moves from 83 % to 90 % with modules while
   K2, K4 and K5 hardly move: they cannot host the modules (no knife on a free axis, no plunge, no yaw,
   no room in the bore).
4. **The food result is decided by sequence and fat more than by mechanism.** Six of the eight B1 walk-
   throughs make Rouladen gravy without browning onion and tomato paste in the fond; five fry Wiener
   Schnitzel in 0.2–1.3 mm of fat (the traditional crust needs 3–4 mm and the 250 mL rule allows it); four
   slice freshly boiled, warm potatoes for Bratkartoffeln or tumble raw slices; three have no core probe and
   fry mince and steak "by time". These are cheap to fix and expensive if found by the MEAL-015 panel.
5. **The best coverage per unit of complexity comes from modules, not cells**: a press tube with end plates,
   turning hob positions with a hung scraper and kneading roller, a fork-and-peeler (spit) with a knife and
   comb, the G-produce set (garlic press cup, pepper plug corer and four-cheek cut, snipper basket, cone cut,
   dunk basket) and one core probe. Round 2 should build the hybrid from K6 or K1 as the manipulator base,
   K7's cold plate as a booster, K3's drum as the stir-fry and bulk-peel station, and K4's mat only as a
   sheeting-and-rolling station (section 10).

## 9. Timing, quantities and hot-holding (summary of sections 3 and 4)

* **Menus with a fried component and a slow side** (B2, reference menus with Schnitzel or Frikadellen):

### 10.3 Recipe and sequence rules to write into the recipe format now (all concepts)

1. Meat for braising is seared in batches, taken out, onion and tomato paste are roasted in the fond, then
   deglazed; mince is seared first in a wide hot pan, vegetables after.
2. Fried and breaded items finish last; breading ≤ 10 min before frying; 200–250 mL of fat for Schnitzel
   and fish, turned by lift rack or singly by turner, never by an inverted pan with fat in the joint.
3. Bratkartoffeln and potato salad from potatoes boiled in the skin, cooled (cold plate or cold storage),
   then sliced.
4. Rouladen: mustard stops 35 mm before the flap edge; the flap is salted and floured; seam seared first,
   untouched; then raft or pins.
5. Leaves are washed and spun alone; cut vegetables are added after spinning.
6. Every steak, roast, mince and poultry item is finished by core temperature.
