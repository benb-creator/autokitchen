# K2b — Vessel stack and inversion, improved

Round P5a improvement of K2 (`concepts/K2-vessel-stack-inversion.md`). Inputs: the K2 document, the K2
findings and round-2 recommendations of C1–C5, the decision matrix, sections 1, 2, 4, 11 and 12 of K1, K3,
K4, K5, K6 and K8 (and the finished K8b), the sub-mechanism inventory of the catalogue, the gap documents
G-produce and G-assembly-meat, DECISIONS #1–#28. Nothing was built. Tags: **[C]** calculated here,
**[E]** engineering estimate, **[K]** known practice, **[U]** unverified, **[S]** from a source document.
Dimensions in mm; X along the wall from the left inner wall, Y from the rear inner wall towards the door,
z from the room floor.

**Core idea kept:** all food lives in passive ware with **one round rim**; work is done in **closed
stacks** (vessel / interposer disc / vessel); one carriage with a **roll axis and weighing jaws** carries,
clamps, inverts, tumbles, drains and unmoulds. **What changed most:** the jaws became a centring V-groove
clamp that also holds every tool by a small second neck (so the Wender now picks, places, cuts and peels);
all hot work is on one level at 0.9 m; the press column, the tip cradle, the hourglass dock, the stirring
spindle, the stacked hob tiers, the wash lathe with its dry flash and the sideways oven are gone; force now
comes as **torque from one canned hub under a heated turning station**, worked against tools that hang on a
fixed anchor post. 21 → 6 motion actuators, 8 → 4 dynamic seals.

## 2. The improved concept

### 2.1 Definition in one page

K2b is a closed stainless cell **1350 wide × 600 deep × 2000 high** with one deck at z 900 and three
columns side by side. Behind them, in a **rear lane** 115 mm deep, the **Wender** travels: an X carriage in
a dry box at the top of the rear wall, a mast hanging from it, a Z carriage, a sealed roll unit turning
about Y, and one pair of jaws that reach forward from the roll unit to the **ware line** at Y 335. Nothing
of the Wender is ever above an open vessel: the jaws hold a vessel at its neck, below the pouring edge,
from both sides, and the roll unit stays behind it.

* **Column A (X 0–360): wash lift W.** A wash chamber below the deck (commercial undercounter wash
  hardware, 85 °C rinse), whose mouth in the deck is closed by one lid leaf. Ware is set on a loose rack;
  the Wender lowers the rack into the chamber by a handle and lifts the lid leaf. With the lid open the
  mouth is also the sink, the drain for cooking water, the waste strainer, the jaw rinse and the place
  over which risky inversions happen. Above it: a passive **tool carousel** (12 stem tools) and the
  **box landing** under the transport port in the ceiling.
* **Column B (X 360–690): turning station KH.** A glass-ceramic plate with a ring induction coil
  (2.5 kW) around a **canned coaxial hub**: slow drive T (30 Nm, 0–600 rpm) and fast drive S (1.5 Nm,
  0–6000 rpm) work through welded thimbles, no seal. On T a pot turns under a hung scraper or kneading
  roller, a perforated disc mills, a spit turns produce against a held blade, a plate turns for polar
  plating. On S the blender jug, chopper cup and whisk cup run. A fixed **anchor post** at the front-left
  corner takes every reaction torque and carries the drinking-water spout. Three load cells weigh the
  station. The space above KH is kept free.
* **Column C (X 690–1290): bought 60 cm flex hob** with two flex zones, under-mounted flush with the deck
  (H1, H2). Above it, behind an insulated sloped soffit, the **clean-air ware store**.

Everything that touches food is loose ware with one of two grip features: the **R-rim** of vessels, discs
and trays (flange Ø 290, neck Ø 266, 20° Tri-Clamp back), or the **T45 neck** of tools (collar Ø 45 with
the same V-groove). The jaws grip both. The oven stands outside the cell (O1), a household oven used as
bought with a driven door, loaded by the transport system through the box landing. Prep and cooking ware
is washed in W during and after the meal; plates and glasses go to the household dishwasher of the washing
module.

* **R-rim pocket.** Each finger carries an arc of R 133 over ±50° with a 20° V-groove (the section of a
  Tri-Clamp, the hygienic standard for rim joints). Closing the fingers on the necks of a mated pair pushes
  both flanges into the groove: radial force F becomes axial clamping force F / tan(20° + 8.5°) ≈ 1.8 F
  per finger [C, µ 0.15], and both rims are centred on the same arcs, whatever the play of the carriage.
  The two arcs cover 200° of the circumference. A single vessel is held positively in both axial
  directions by its neck (flange above, bead below).
* **T45 notch.** At the bottom of each arc a notch R 22.5 with the same groove takes the **T45 neck**
  that every tool carries. The fingers open to ±26 to approach a tool, so tools may stand 70 mm apart.
* **Straight V-slots** either side of the arc take GN flanges (GN 1/2 short side, 265 or 325) and the
  flanges of GN storage boxes.
* **What one jaw pair replaces:** jaw A, jaw B and the squeeze axis of K2 (−2 actuators); the tool

## 3. How the hard operations work now

### 3.1 Six principles that replace the missing hand

1. **Polar work on T.** The turntable angle θ and the Wender's X give every point (r, θ) of a Ø 300 disc
   on the ware line; Z gives the height, roll the tilt. A board, a plate, a pizza pan or a tray on the T
   plug is fully reachable without a Y axis (K4, K8b). Orientation of a piece is set by θ as well.
2. **T turns, the Wender holds.** Cutting power comes from T (30 Nm); the Wender only positions a blade,
   corer, spoon or rasp and feeds it with 5–100 N. Cutting reactions are tangential, i.e. along Y at the
   contact point on the ware line: the V-notch holds the tool's neck in Y and the mast is stiff (2.2).
3. **Roll-axis tools.** A part mounted coaxially with the roll axis in front of the T45 neck turns about
   its own axis: rolling pin (driven at surface speed v = ω r, no bearing, K8), dough wheel, dealer roller
   (GA-24), roll spit.
4. **Pour about the lip.** X, Z and roll form a planar chain with three degrees of freedom; the lip of the
   source vessel is kept on a virtual pivot 10 mm inside the target mouth for the whole tilt. Rate is
   closed-loop on the KH load cells or the jaw load pins (± 5 g). This replaces K2's tip cradle and makes
   every merge possible (K3's lip pivot in software).
5. **Tongs by the jaw stroke.** One-piece sprung U-tongs; each arm carries half a T45 neck; finger
   position = tong opening, finger current = grip force (2–40 N at the tips). Release into a holster that
   pinches the arms. No joint, no second drive (K6).
6. **Stacks and pairs** (K2's own): flip, unmould, closed toss and coat, drain through a strainer disc,
   transfer of sticky masses; all built on the station and turned at z ≤ 1200 over the hob, KH or the
   open wash lift. Hot liquids are never inverted.

### 3.3 Dice an onion

Peeled onions are bought (#9, baseline). **Dice:** the chimney bowl with the **bought dicing kit** stands on
KH, its tab in the anchor fork; T drives the slicing disc at 300–375 rpm through the chimney (Robot Coupe
dicing runs at 375 rpm [K]). The Wender drops one onion at a time into the feed tube with the tongs and
presses the pusher cup with 20–60 N; the disc cuts a 10 mm slice and the ejector pushes it through the
10 mm grid. 500 g in about 40 s [E]. The bowl is then poured into the pan about its lip. **Fine onion**
(Frikadellen, sauces): S-cup, three pulses of 1 s at 3000 rpm, 3–5 mm. Confidence H (household dicing kits
dice onions; some layers separate, as by hand).

### 3.4 Rouladen: fill, roll, secure, sear, braise (4 persons, 4 large Rouladen; 8 with a second raft)

1. **Slices**: butcher-cut slices (#8), shingled or interleaved in the opened pack (request). The pack
   carrier stands on T; the roll-driven **dealer roller** pushes the top slice over the pack edge onto the
   peel, or the peel goes under a shingled edge. The peel lays each slice on the Rouladen apron in a
   GN 1/2-20 on the board carrier; θ sets its orientation (long axis along X). Flattening, if needed:
   rolling pin through a silicone cover sheet, 2 N/mm line load (200–250 N).
2. **Fill** (C1 §10.3 rule 4): mustard spooned and spread by the spatula, **the last 35 mm of the flap
   left free, salted and dusted with flour** (spoon tap); bacon strip (dealer or tongs), onion strips and a
   gherkin strip placed by tongs at polar positions.
3. **Side folds and roll**: the apron tray has two plough rails (GA-04) that turn the long edges in by
   15 mm as the roll forms. The Wender takes the apron's hem bar by its T45 neck and draws it over the
   slices in an X–Z arc (cloth method: two slices per pass, K6/K8). The roll stops when the camera has seen
   the measured slice length wound; the rolls tip seam-down into the comb cradle, flap at 5 o'clock.
4. **Secure — GA-20 raft**: the two-tine fork (tines 6 × 1.5 on edge, 60 apart) is pushed **in X** through
   the guide slots of the cradle and through all four rolls (30–60 N [S]). The cradle stays; the raft is a
   rigid unit with its T45 handle on a stem at the +X end.
5. **Sear**: PN 1 on H1, 20 mL fat at 200 °C; the raft goes in seam-down for 90 s untouched, then the roll
   turns it over (lift 100, roll 180° about the handle, lower; 2.2 Nm [S]); about 60 % of the surface
   browns (two broad faces) instead of one strip in a channel.
6. **Fond and braise** (C1 A-2): raft out onto the platter disc; onion dice and tomato paste roasted in the
   fat for 4 min with the paddle; red wine poured about the lip and reduced; stock; raft back, lid on;
   H1 at simmer for 90 min — on the hob, so the oven is not needed. The pot stays lidded; no stirring.
7. **Gravy**: raft out; PN 1 (a T-pan) goes to KH; the hung scraper turns the gravy while flour slurry is
   poured; 3 min.
8. **Serve**: the raft is held over the plate; a stripper comb on the cradle pushes the rolls off one by
   one, seam down (1–3 N [S]).

Confidence: raft holding H [S: G-assembly 4.2]; rolling M (shared untested step of all concepts); seam
angle M. Fallback: single pins (SM-085) set by the tongs through the cradle notches, counted out by the
camera; one large Roulade (adapted).

### 3.5 Breaded Schnitzel in 3–4 mm of fat (4 persons)

* **Breading** (≈ 5 min for four): cutlets (bought thin, or rolled to 5 mm between silicone sheets) and

  egg whisked in the S-cup and poured into a third tray on W's closed lid; the U-tongs dip each cutlet,
  turn it by roll, let it drain 5 s, lay it in the crumb tray on H2 (cold), crumbs spooned over, pressed
  at 30 N with the platter disc, turned by tongs. Breading ≤ 10 min before frying (C1 rule 2).
* **Frying**: PN 1 on H1 and PN 2 on KH, each with **160 mL clarified butter = 3.4 mm** over the Ø 240
  floor [C], 170 °C. The lower GA-36 rack (Ø 235 on 5 mm feet) lies in the fat; two cutlets per pan are
  laid on it by the tongs. After 3 min the upper rack is set on top (ears side by side); the jaws grip both
  ear pairs in the straight slots, lift 60 mm, hold 5 s over the pan (the fat drips back), roll 180° in
  1 s, lower. 3 min. The pair is lifted out, drained, the upper rack lifted off; the cutlets go to the
  plate on the peel. **No fat ever sits in a rim joint** (G-assembly 7.1). The crust can puff because the
  cutlet floats in 3.4 mm of fat. Cost: faint rack marks (M–H). Fried items finish last; hold ≤ 5 min.

### 3.11 Carving

**Parting on T** (K2's own sickle carving, made a draw cut): the rested boneless roast stands on end on the
3-tine spit plate (spit only 30 mm long). The Wender holds a wide flat **parting blade** (150 × 120, edge
horizontal along X) at height z = top − t and feeds it inward in X at 2–3 mm/s while T turns at 30 rpm:
at r = 50 mm the edge slides at 0.16 m/s, a slice-to-push ratio of about 60, so the normal force drops by
3–5 × and soft braised meat does not tear [S: G-assembly 3.2]. The parted slice lies on the blade; the
Wender carries it to the plate and the GA-01 stripper bar slides it off. Z steps down by t (4–6 mm firm,
8–10 mm braised). About 25 s per slice; the bottom 30 mm stay on the spit (end piece). Cake portions: radial

### 3.12 Plating 2–4 portions nicely

Warm plates (from the serving module, two per carrier through the landing) go onto the plate carrier on T.
For each plate a **polar layout**: meat at 6 o'clock (peel, tongs or the raft's stripper), starch at 10
(mash as a neat dome by the disher, potatoes by ladle with a slotted bowl), vegetables at 2 (slotted ladle),
**sauce poured as an arc** from BK about its lip while T turns 90°, garnish by the spoon tap. Optional ring
collar (GA-01) for stacked builds. About 60 s per plate [E]; two plates leave per carrier, the first pair
after 2.5 min, all four in 4.5 min — SRV-009 (3 min from first to last plate) is met only if the serving

## 5. Cleaning

### 5.1 Principle and zones

**Zone F is ware only.** Everything that touches food leaves its station and goes through W. Fixed items
next to food: the spout tip on the anchor post (drinking water only, drip-free valve), the KH and hob glass
(touched only by vessel bases), the T plug (ware, washed in W after every meal). **Zone S**: deck, rear lane
with mast and bands, the jaw fingers and their open screw, anchor post, store soffit, walls, ceiling, door,
box landing. Nothing of the Wender is above an open vessel (C2 R-2): the roll unit and the screw stay
behind the vessel, the fingers touch necks below the pouring edge.

### 5.2 The wash lift W

| Step | Medium | Time | Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold pre-rinse (protein and starch leave cold, C2 R-7) | mains, to drain through the strainer | 12 s | 0.8 L |
| Wash | tank 60 °C, alkaline detergent 2 g/L, 10 wall jets + floor spray head, recirculated | 100 s | — |
| Drain back | | 10 s | — |
| Fresh rinse, 85 °C, rinse aid | from the 8 L boiler, runs into the tank | 20 s | 2.2 L |
| **Class R loads: hot hold** | rinse water recirculated at ≥ 82 °C on the surface (logger on the coldest item, C2 R-3); A0 ≥ 60 | **+ 60 s** | + 1.0 L |
| Dry | lid ajar, extraction draws through the chamber; steel at ≈ 75 °C dries by its own heat | 45 s | — |
| **Total** | | **3.1 min; 4.1 min class R** | **3.0 L; 4.0 L** |

* **Load**: one R-vessel inverted on the three-point stand, up to two discs on the disc rest beside the
  post, up to four tools in the post's comb (T45 necks in saddles). The Wender raises the rack by its handle;
  a cam on the post lifts the lid leaf with it and lets it close when the rack goes down (no lid event).
* **One-way flow** (C2 R-6): clean items leave the rack before soiled ones go in; clean ware goes to the
  filtered-air store or to its next station, never back over the open chamber.
* **Wet parking and cold rinse within 2 min** (C2 R-7): an emptied hot vessel is held 6 s in the cold fan
  jets of the mouth and set into the rack or, if the rack is busy, parked inverted on the open mouth grid.
* **Jaw hygiene** (C2 K2-3): after every grip of a class R item the fingers dip into the mouth jets:
  5 s of 85 °C water from the boiler line, then air. Fingers touch only necks.
* **Verification** (C2 R-11): the mouth camera (white and UV-A) sees every item as the rack rises; a
  failed item gets one long cycle (15 min, enzymatic), then quarantine on the landing for the human.
  Riboflavin and dried-soil tests on R-ware, discs, the dicing grid and the mill disc **before** K2b is
  selected.
* **Throughput**: 4–5 loads during a 4-person meal while the hob runs (the boiler heats in KH pauses,
  phase L3), the rest 10–15 min after serving. PERF-005 (next meal after 30 min) is met because the
  frequent items exist twice (PN, P, knife, tongs, board).

### 5.3 The cell after the meal

Fixed nozzle bars: one in the face of the X dry box spraying down the rear lane and the mast, one along
the store soffit edge, two at KH (glass, pocket, anchor post), one over the hob; the day's tank liquor first,
then a fresh rinse (K6), deck gutter to the drain, fan dry 20 min. Hob and KH glass: squeegee and a
stem-held glass scraper for burnt spots. The T plug is lifted out and washed in W; the pocket is flushed at
its drain and checked by the camera. Weekly: the jaw screw and guide bars are flushed with detergent; the
sealing bands are inspected.

### 5.6 Raw and ready-to-eat, waste

* **By instance**: red and green knife, tongs and board; raw meat is handled in PN 1, PN 2 and the GN
  breading trays, which always get the class R wash. **By order** ("dry before wet, RTE before raw"): the
  dicing kit, S-cup and mill serve vegetables and RTE items before any raw meat touches them; when that
  order cannot be kept, a class R wash (4 min) is inserted.
* **Unwashed produce is class R** (C2 R-5): it is washed in W's bath in the basket before it touches a
  green tool or the board.
* **Waste**: skins, cores, peel, shells and the mill's residue go to W's 2 mm strainer basket, which is
  tipped into a closed bio-waste box on the landing for the transport; frying fat into a lidded beaker.
  Waste never passes over an open RTE vessel: it travels at the lowest transit height straight to W.

## 8. Best ideas for the combined machine (K9b)

1. **V-groove (Tri-Clamp) jaws with a tool notch.** One pair of fingers clamps and *centres* a rim-to-rim
   pair (flip, unmould, closed toss, lift-rack turn), carries vessels, GN trays and boxes, and holds every
   tool by a small V-groove neck; the jaw stroke itself closes U-tongs, the disher and the egg cradle; load
   pins in the finger roots check every grip. One actuator does what K2 needed three for and what a hand
   concept needs a tool changer for — a candidate to compare with K9's EPM chuck.
2. **Turning station = ring induction coil around a canned coaxial hub, plus one anchor post.** A heated pot
   turns under a hung scraper, a kneading roller or a mill blade whose reaction arm rests on a fixed post;
   the manipulator is free while it stirs, kneads or mills; zero seals; the same spot is the lathe, the
   blender base and the plating carousel.
3. **Food mill on the turntable plus a bought processor dicing kit through a chimney bowl.** Torque from
   below replaces the press entirely: dice, slice, grate, rice, passata, Spätzle into the simmering pot,
   and "mill-skinning" of soft produce (potato in skin, banana, avocado, cooked pumpkin, tomato). All
   household practice, all ware.
4. **"T turns, the hand holds"** for produce and carving: peel, core, wedge, cone-cut and pare à vif on a
   vertical spit; carve by **parting** a roast on end with a horizontal blade fed inward (draw ratio ≈ 60);
   long produce on a roll-axis spit against a sprung blade post.
5. **Pour about a virtual lip pivot, and never invert hot liquid.** X, Z and roll keep the lip inside the
   target mouth — merges and transfers without a cradle; pairs are inverted only with solid food at
   z ≤ 1200, metal to metal when hot, so nothing pressurises.

1. **One washer for everything (C5 Q1)?** Yes removes W, −360 mm, −€1,250, −23 events per meal, and makes
   the cell ≈ 1000 mm wide; ware then travels on the transport.
2. **Oven O1 (outside, transport) or O2 (countertop in the cell above the hob, drop-down door as the
   loading shelf)?** O2 needs a model with a drop-down door and ≥ 35 L [U] and costs the ware store.
3. Can the transport carry a **GN 2/3 carrier** (one R-vessel, a GN 1/2 tray or two plates)? K2b's oven,
   fixture and plate traffic depend on it.
4. **Serving**: plates out through the landing (baseline) or a guard-locked front flap at KH?
5. **Garlic**: peeled cloves (requirements 5.6) or whole bulbs? Whole bulbs are broken by the pusher cup on
   the board (100–200 N) and the cloves shake-peeled in a closed beaker pair tumbled by the roll at 3 Hz,
   ±60° (≈ 2 g at the wall [C]) — M; skins go to W.
6. Is the **raft** accepted as "secured by the machine" for Rouladen (MEAL-005, UO-42)? It is the skewer of
   the home cook (G-assembly 4.2).
7. Banana **slices** (DS10): accept the fruit salad without banana as class (c), or ask the produce round for a
   slit-and-pull tool?
